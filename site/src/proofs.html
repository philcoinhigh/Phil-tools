The proof ledger

Most languages are tested. Phil is being proved: its rules are written out as theorems and checked by a machine, line by line, in the Rocq prover. This page counts those theorems once a day, straight from Phil's repository.

    What the proofs take on trust

    A proof can be left unfinished and marked Admitted, or it can lean on an Axiom: a statement accepted without proof. Phil's own rule is that neither counts as proved unless it is declared. So we count both.

      How this is produced, and what it does not show

      Everything above is counted by proofs/run.ts, which searches the text of the .v files under proof/ at each day's last commit. A theorem is a line that opens with Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, Proposition, Fact or Remark.

      We do not run the prover ourselves. Phil's own build does that on every change, and it is the authority on whether a proof holds. A count also says nothing about what a theorem is worth: one deep result can matter more than a hundred small ones, and many of these are small steps towards larger ones. Phil's proof notes say which claims each slice does and does not make. This page is independent of the Phil project.
